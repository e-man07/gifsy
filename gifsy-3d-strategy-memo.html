The 3D Wedge

Where the depth feature actually creates value — the one position nobody else holds, the ICP it fits, and the use cases that follow from it.

28 Aug 2026 Research base · 6 products, 6 segments Status · for discussion

There's one box on the competitive map nobody occupies, and it's the one you're in: your own photo → an interactive, web-native embed, in seconds, no modeling, no code. The value is a premium-signal, not a function — it makes a flat photo look expensively art-directed. It wins on attention and polish, and loses the moment it's sold as a conversion, inspection, or animation tool.

Recommended ICP

The no-code / low-code site-builder making marketing & portfolio sites — whose real job is “make me look more capable than my budget.”

One person, seen twice. The two 4/5 segments are the same buyer: the site-builder with a real photo, a premium itch, and zero 3D skill. That's the exact “someone building a website ships it” persona set as the bar for this rework — the research landed there on its own.

$

The value is already priced. Your ICP pays $12–14/mo for the adjacent thing today (Spline $12, Unicorn Studio $14) on top of their builder plan. You're contesting an existing budget line, not inventing one.

What the feature is for

Every case below is the same move — take the real photo they already have and make it look art-directed, in one embed, without a 3D artist. Ranked by strength.

  1. 1

    The premium hero

    The client's real product shot, brand key-art, or founder portrait becomes a hover- and scroll-reactive depth hero above the fold.

    The flagship — exactly what Spline can't do without modeling the product
  2. 2

    The “expensive” About / team page

    A founder or team portrait with subtle depth reads bespoke and high-end on a page that's normally flat.

  3. 3

    Portfolio & case-study thumbnails

    Gallery images that come alive on hover — motion that signals quality without a Spline scene or a dev.

  4. 4

    Category heroes

    Architecture & interiors → a building shot; hospitality → a signature dish; SaaS → a product screenshot or key-art given depth above the fold.

  5. 5

    The pitch-winner

    The designer drops an interactive mockup into a staging site to win the client — “look what we can do” — at near-zero effort. A use case for the designer's own sales process; maybe the stickiest hook of all.

05

Where we stand on first value

The aha isn't in your editor — it's the working embed on their real page.
Repeated across five of six segments

First value is “I pasted one line into my Webflow hero, moved my mouse, and my actual photo lifted off its background — live.” The in-app preview is not the activation event.

And it gates on one thing that is pure product, not marketing: artifact quality on the user's own, imperfect photo. This ICP is the least forgiving audience alive — they judge the effect on their own showcase image. A first upload with hair, a busy background, or weak subject/background separation produces an anti-aha: a halo or stretched edge that permanently sours them.

That is exactly what Phase 1 of the rework attacked — edge-aware depth refinement, halo removal, disocclusion inpaint. The research independently confirms that was the right, and gating, investment. For this ICP it isn't polish; it's the whole game.

✓

Net position. An uncontested wedge, aimed at a real ICP that already pays for the adjacent thing, with adoption gated almost entirely on first-upload quality — the precise thing you've been fixing.

!

The honest caveat

Depth-parallax has a 20-year record as a front door, not a foundation — a first-session wow that decays to wallpaper by the tenth view, with no recurring job (Facebook 3D Photos, Depthy, LeiaPix all peaked and faded).

Treat the interactive embed as the hook and edge quality as the moat. Winning the ICP's first value doesn't require answering “why do they come back?” — but that unanswered question is what decides whether this is a great feature or a company.
